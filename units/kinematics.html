Kinematics

Describing motion without asking what causes it. Scalars vs vectors, graphs, 1D and 2D motion, free fall, projectiles, and relative motion.

Scalars and Vectors

Tip

Never add a scalar to a vector. If an equation mixes them, it is wrong. Always check units and quantity type before solving.

Distance and Displacement

Common mistake

Treating distance and displacement as interchangeable. On a round trip distance is positive; displacement is zero.

Speed and Velocity

v_avg = Δx / Δt speed_avg = total distance / total time v = dx/dt

Acceleration

Common mistake

Thinking acceleration is only “speeding up.” An object moving right while slowing down has leftward acceleration. Deceleration is not a separate vector; it is acceleration opposite velocity.

Motion Graphs

Exam tip

Sketch the graph even when the problem does not ask for one. It reveals whether velocity is constant, when the object stops, and the sign of acceleration.

Constant-Acceleration Equations

v = v₀ + at x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at² v² = v₀² + 2aΔx x = x₀ + ½(v₀ + v)t

Example

Car from rest, a = 3.0 m/s², t = 5.0 s. x = ½(3.0)(25) = 37.5 m ≈ 38 m (2 sig figs). Check: units m/s² × s² = m.

Problem-solving order

1. Draw a sketch and choose a positive direction. 2. List knowns/unknowns with signs. 3. Pick equation. 4. Solve symbols first. 5. Substitute and check units and reasonableness.

Free Fall

Common misconception

“Heavier objects fall faster.” In vacuum they do not. Air resistance makes a crumpled paper fall faster than a flat sheet; mass itself is not the cause.

Projectile Motion

v₀ₓ = v₀ cos θ    v₀ᵧ = v₀ sin θ R = (v₀² sin 2θ)/g    (level ground) H = (v₀ sin θ)²/(2g) T = 2 v₀ sin θ / g

Tip

Maximum range on level ground is at 45°. Complementary angles (30° and 60°) give the same range but different times of flight and max heights.
