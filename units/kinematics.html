02 — Kinematics

Describing motion without worrying about the forces that cause it. Position, velocity, acceleration, and the equations that connect them.

Distance vs Displacement

ELI5

Distance is how much ground you covered walking around. Displacement is the straight-line arrow from where you started to where you ended up — it has a direction.

High School

Distance is a scalar: the total path length traveled. Displacement is a vector: the change in position, Δx = x_final − x_initial. Displacement depends only on the start and end points, not the path taken.

Professional

Distance is the scalar arc length of the trajectory. Displacement is the vector difference between the final and initial position vectors, independent of the path. In one dimension it reduces to a signed quantity; in higher dimensions it is a true vector in Euclidean space.

Speed vs Velocity

ELI5

Speed is how fast you’re going. Velocity is how fast you’re going and which way. “60 mph north” is velocity; “60 mph” is just speed.

High School

Speed is the scalar rate of change of distance. Average speed = total distance / total time. Velocity is the vector rate of change of displacement. Average velocity = Δx / Δt. Instantaneous velocity is the limit of average velocity as Δt → 0 (the derivative dx/dt).

Professional

Speed is the magnitude of the velocity vector. Instantaneous velocity is the time derivative of the position vector, v = dr/dt. Average velocity over an interval is the displacement divided by the elapsed time. The distinction between average and instantaneous quantities becomes essential when acceleration is non-zero.

v_avg = Δx / Δt speed_avg = total distance / total time v = dx/dt (instantaneous)

Acceleration

ELI5

Acceleration is how quickly your velocity is changing. Speeding up, slowing down, or turning all count as acceleration because your velocity vector is changing.

High School

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity: a = Δv / Δt. It is a vector. Speeding up in the direction of velocity means acceleration and velocity have the same sign (1D). Slowing down means they have opposite signs. Constant acceleration produces the four kinematic equations.

Professional

Acceleration is the second time derivative of position (or first derivative of velocity): a = dv/dt = d²r/dt². When acceleration is constant, integration yields the standard kinematic relations. In general, acceleration may have components both parallel (tangential) and perpendicular (centripetal) to the velocity.

a_avg = Δv / Δt a = dv/dt = d²x/dt²

The Four Kinematic Equations (Constant Acceleration)

ELI5

If something speeds up or slows down at a steady rate, these four magic formulas let you find any missing piece — how far it went, how fast it ended up, how long it took — as long as you know three other things.

High School

When acceleration is constant, the following equations relate position, velocity, acceleration, and time. You never need all five variables; any equation uses only four of them. Choose the equation that does not contain the variable you don’t know and don’t need.

Professional

Under the assumption of constant acceleration, the first-order differential equation a = dv/dt integrates directly to v = v₀ + at. A second integration yields x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at². Elimination of time or acceleration between these relations produces the remaining two equations. These form a complete algebraic set for one-dimensional motion with constant a.

v = v₀ + at x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at² v² = v₀² + 2a(x − x₀) x = x₀ + ½(v₀ + v)t

Worked Example

A car starts from rest and accelerates at 3.0 m/s² for 5.0 s. How far does it travel?

v₀ = 0, a = 3.0 m/s², t = 5.0 s → x = 0 + 0 + ½(3)(25) = 37.5 m ≈ 38 m (2 sig figs).

Free Fall

ELI5

When something is only being pulled by gravity (no engines, no parachutes), it falls with the same acceleration no matter how heavy it is — about 9.8 m/s every second faster toward the ground.

High School

Free fall means the only force acting is gravity (air resistance neglected). Acceleration is g ≈ 9.80 m/s² downward, constant near Earth’s surface. All kinematic equations apply with a = −g (if up is positive). Objects thrown upward slow down, stop, then speed up downward; the velocity at the same height going up and down has equal magnitude and opposite sign.

Professional

In the uniform gravitational field approximation valid near Earth’s surface, the acceleration of free fall is the constant vector g directed toward the local vertical. The equations of motion reduce to the constant-acceleration kinematic set with a = g. The independence of mass follows directly from the equivalence of inertial and gravitational mass.

g ≈ 9.80 m/s² downward Same equations with a = ±g

Projectile Motion (2D)

ELI5

Throw a ball at an angle. The horizontal part of its motion stays steady (no sideways forces). The vertical part acts exactly like free fall. The curved path is just those two motions happening at the same time.

High School

Projectile motion decomposes into independent horizontal and vertical components. Horizontal: aₓ = 0 → vₓ = v₀ cos θ (constant). Vertical: aᵧ = −g → same free-fall equations. Time of flight, range, and maximum height are derived by combining the two components. The trajectory is a parabola.

Professional

In the absence of air resistance the equations of motion separate in Cartesian coordinates. Horizontal motion is uniform; vertical motion is uniformly accelerated by g. The parametric equations x(t) = (v₀ cos θ)t and y(t) = (v₀ sin θ)t − ½gt² may be combined to eliminate t, yielding the parabolic trajectory equation y(x).

v₀ₓ = v₀ cos θ    v₀ᵧ = v₀ sin θ Range R = (v₀² sin 2θ) / g    (level ground) Max height H = (v₀ sin θ)² / (2g) Time of flight T = (2 v₀ sin θ) / g
