11 — Magnetism

Magnetic fields, forces on charges and currents, and electromagnetic induction.

Magnetic Fields

ELI5

A magnetic field is the invisible influence around a magnet or a current-carrying wire. It pushes on other magnets and on moving electric charges.

High School

Magnetic field B is a vector field. Direction is the way a compass needle points (north). Field lines form closed loops; they never begin or end. Unit: tesla (T). Earth has a weak magnetic field (~10⁻⁵ T).

Professional

The magnetic field is defined via the Lorentz force law. It is a pseudovector field satisfying ∇·B = 0 (no magnetic monopoles) and ∇×B = μ₀J + μ₀ε₀∂E/∂t (Ampère–Maxwell law). The SI unit is the tesla.

Force on a Moving Charge

ELI5

A magnetic field only pushes on charges that are already moving, and the push is sideways — perpendicular to both the velocity and the field.

High School

F = qvB sinθ. Direction by right-hand rule (for positive charge). When v ⊥ B the path is a circle of radius r = mv/(qB). Magnetic force does no work because it is always perpendicular to velocity.

Professional

The magnetic part of the Lorentz force is F = q v × B. Because F · v = 0 the magnetic force does no work and leaves kinetic energy unchanged. The resulting motion in a uniform field is a helix of constant pitch and cyclotron frequency ω = qB/m.

F = qvB sinθ r = mv / (qB)

Force on a Current-Carrying Wire

ELI5

A wire with current is full of moving charges, so a magnetic field can push on the whole wire.

High School

F = ILB sinθ. Direction again by right-hand rule. Parallel currents attract; antiparallel currents repel.

Professional

A current element Idl experiences dF = I dl × B. For a straight wire of length L this integrates to F = IL × B. The force between two parallel currents is the microscopic origin of the definition of the ampere in older SI formulations.

F = ILB sinθ

Electromagnetic Induction

ELI5

Changing a magnetic field through a loop of wire creates an electric current. Moving a magnet near a coil or changing the current in a nearby coil both work.

High School

Faraday’s law: ε = −dΦ_B/dt, where magnetic flux Φ_B = BA cosθ. Lenz’s law: the induced current creates a field that opposes the change in flux. Motional emf: ε = Bℓv for a rod sliding on rails.

Professional

Faraday’s law in integral form states that the emf around a closed loop equals the negative rate of change of magnetic flux through any surface bounded by the loop. In differential form it is ∇×E = −∂B/∂t. Lenz’s law is the manifestation of energy conservation (the induced current’s magnetic field opposes the change).

ε = −dΦ_B / dt Φ_B = BA cosθ ε = Bℓv (motional)
