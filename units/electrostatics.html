Electrostatics

Charge, Coulomb’s law, fields, potential, energy, and conductors.

Electric Charge

e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C

Induction tip

Bring a charged object near a conductor → charges separate. Ground the conductor → opposite charge flows away (or toward). Remove ground, then remove the charged object → conductor is left charged opposite to the inducer.

Coulomb’s Law

F = k|q₁q₂|/r² k = 9.0 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²

Common mistake

Forgetting that force is a vector. Two forces of equal magnitude at an angle do not simply add numerically — resolve into components.

Electric Field

E = F/q E = kq/r²    (point charge)

Tip

E is independent of the test charge. Direction is defined via a positive test charge. Negative charges feel force opposite to E.

Electric Potential

V = kq/r ΔV = Ed    (uniform) U = qV = kq₁q₂/r

Conductors in Electrostatics

Exam tip

E = 0 inside the metal, not necessarily inside a hollow cavity if charge is enclosed. External static fields are shielded from the interior of a conducting shell.

Capacitance (Brief)

C = Q/V C = κε₀A/d U = ½CV²
