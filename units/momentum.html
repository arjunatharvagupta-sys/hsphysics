Momentum & Collisions

Momentum is conserved in isolated systems. Impulse links force and time to Δp. Collisions are classified by whether kinetic energy is conserved.

Linear Momentum

p = mv

Impulse–Momentum Theorem

J = FΔt = Δp

Example

A 0.15 kg baseball going 40 m/s is stopped in 0.020 s. Δp = 6.0 kg·m/s. Average force = Δp/Δt = 300 N.

Conservation of Momentum

Σ m vᵢ = Σ m v_f    (isolated)

Strategy

During a collision, external forces (gravity, normal) often impulse much less than the huge internal collision forces. Treat the system as isolated for the collision instant, then use energy or kinematics before/after if needed.

Elastic vs Inelastic Collisions

Perfectly inelastic: m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁+m₂)v_f Elastic 1D (equal mass): velocities exchange

Common mistakes

• Using KE conservation when the collision is not elastic.
• Forgetting that momentum is a vector — directions (signs) matter.
• Applying “velocities exchange” when masses are unequal.
