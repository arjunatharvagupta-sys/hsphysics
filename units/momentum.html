05 — Momentum & Collisions

Momentum is “mass in motion.” It is conserved in isolated systems and is the key to analyzing collisions and explosions.

Linear Momentum

ELI5

Momentum tells you how hard it is to stop something. A heavy truck moving slowly can have the same momentum as a light car moving fast.

High School

Momentum p = mv. It is a vector in the same direction as velocity. Unit: kg·m/s. The direction matters; opposite directions can cancel.

Professional

Linear momentum of a particle is the product of its mass and velocity vector, p = mv. For a system it is the vector sum of the individual momenta and equals M v_cm. It is the generator of spatial translations in Hamiltonian mechanics and is conserved when the net external force vanishes.

p = mv

Impulse

ELI5

Impulse is a force applied for a stretch of time. A big force for a short time (hitting a baseball) or a smaller force for a longer time can give the same impulse and change the momentum by the same amount.

High School

Impulse J = F_avg Δt = Δp. The impulse–momentum theorem links force and time to the change in momentum. Area under an F-versus-t graph equals impulse.

Professional

Impulse is the time integral of force, J = ∫ F dt, and equals the change in momentum by direct integration of Newton’s second law. It is especially useful when forces are large and brief (impulsive forces) so that the detailed time dependence need not be resolved.

J = FΔt = Δp

Conservation of Momentum

ELI5

In a closed system with no outside pushes or pulls, the total momentum before an event equals the total momentum after. Momentum just gets shared or rearranged.

High School

When the net external force on a system is zero, total momentum is conserved: Σpᵢ = Σp_f. This holds for collisions and explosions in isolated systems. Internal forces cancel by Newton’s third law.

Professional

If the net external force on a system vanishes, the total linear momentum is a constant of the motion. This follows at once from dP/dt = ΣF_ext. In the center-of-mass frame the total momentum is identically zero both before and after any internal interaction.

Σ m vᵢ = Σ m v_f    (isolated system)

Collisions: Elastic vs Inelastic

ELI5

In an elastic collision, objects bounce without losing any “bounce energy.” In an inelastic collision some of that energy turns into heat, sound, or deformation. Perfectly inelastic means they stick together.

High School

Elastic: both momentum and kinetic energy conserved. Inelastic: momentum conserved, kinetic energy not. Perfectly inelastic: objects share a common final velocity; maximum kinetic-energy loss. Use momentum conservation first; check KE only if the problem states the collision is elastic.

Professional

A collision is elastic if the total kinetic energy is the same before and after; otherwise it is inelastic. In one dimension the elastic case yields a unique pair of final velocities once masses and initial velocities are given. The coefficient of restitution quantifies the degree of elasticity (e = 1 elastic, e = 0 perfectly inelastic).

Elastic: KE conserved + p conserved Perfectly inelastic: m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁+m₂)v_f
