06 — Circular Motion & Gravity

Moving in circles requires a center-pointing force. Gravity supplies that force for planets, moons, and satellites.

Uniform Circular Motion

ELI5

Even if speed stays the same, direction keeps changing when you go in a circle. Changing direction means acceleration — and that acceleration always points toward the center of the circle.

High School

In uniform circular motion speed is constant but velocity is not, because direction changes continuously. Centripetal acceleration a_c = v²/r = ω²r points toward the center. The required net force is the centripetal force F_c = ma_c = mv²/r.

Professional

Uniform circular motion is characterized by constant angular speed ω and constant radius. The linear velocity is tangential, v = ωr, while the acceleration is purely radial (centripetal), a = −ω²r ê_r. Any force providing this acceleration is called centripetal; it is not a new kind of force but the net radial component of the real forces acting on the particle.

a_c = v²/r = ω²r F_c = mv²/r v = 2πr / T    ω = 2π / T

Newton’s Law of Universal Gravitation

ELI5

Every piece of matter pulls on every other piece of matter. The bigger the masses and the closer they are, the stronger the pull. That’s why we stick to Earth and why the Moon stays in orbit.

High School

F_g = G m₁ m₂ / r², where G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg². The force is always attractive and acts along the line joining the centers. Weight on Earth is the gravitational force exerted by Earth on an object.

Professional

Newton’s law of universal gravitation states that every particle attracts every other particle with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. The gravitational field g(r) = −GM ê_r / r² is conservative; its potential is Φ = −GM/r. The law is the weak-field, low-velocity limit of general relativity.

F_g = G m₁ m₂ / r² G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²

Orbital Motion & Kepler

ELI5

Satellites (and planets) are continuously falling toward the body they orbit, but they are also moving sideways fast enough that they keep missing it. That combination produces a closed orbit.

High School

For a circular orbit the centripetal force is supplied by gravity: GMm/r² = mv²/r → v = √(GM/r). Kepler’s third law for circular orbits: T² ∝ r³. Period increases rapidly with orbital radius.

Professional

Setting the gravitational force equal to the required centripetal force yields the circular-orbit speed. Kepler’s third law follows at once: T² = (4π²/GM) r³. More generally, bound orbits under an inverse-square force are ellipses with the central mass at one focus (Kepler’s first law); equal areas are swept in equal times (second law) by conservation of angular momentum.

v_orbit = √(GM / r) T² = (4π² / GM) r³
