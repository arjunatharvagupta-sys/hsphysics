Measurement & Uncertainty

How we quantify the physical world — precision, error, units, and the rules that keep calculations honest.

Significant Figures

Non-zero digits → always significant Zeros between non-zeros → significant Leading zeros → not significant Trailing zeros with decimal → significant × or ÷ → fewest sig figs + or − → least precise decimal place

Example

Length 12.50 cm (4 sig figs) × width 3.2 cm (2 sig figs) → area = 40 cm² (2 sig figs). The calculator shows 40.000, but the data only support two significant figures.

Common mistakes

Counting leading zeros (0.0045 has two sig figs, not four). Keeping every digit the calculator displays. Mixing the multiplication rule with the addition rule.

Accuracy vs Precision

Uncertainty and Error

Percent uncertainty = (Δx / x) × 100% Percent error = |(x_exp − x_true) / x_true| × 100%

SI Units and Scientific Notation

Base units: m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd Scientific notation: a × 10ⁿ (1 ≤ |a| < 10)
