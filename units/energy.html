Work, Energy & Power

Work transfers energy. Mechanical energy can be conserved. Power is the rate of transfer. Choose energy methods when forces are unknown or path details are messy.

Work

W = Fd cosθ W = ∫ F · dr

Common mistakes

• Thinking any force on a moving object does work (centripetal force does zero work because it is perpendicular to velocity).
• Forgetting cosθ when force and displacement are not parallel.
• Using distance instead of displacement when the force is constant in direction.

Kinetic Energy and the Work–Energy Theorem

KE = ½mv² W_net = ΔKE

Example

A 2.0 kg object speeds from 3.0 m/s to 7.0 m/s. ΔKE = ½(2)(49 − 9) = 40 J. Net work done on it is 40 J.

Potential Energy

PE_g = mgh PE_s = ½kx²

Tip

Set PE = 0 at the lowest point or at the spring’s equilibrium — whichever simplifies the algebra. Consistency within one problem is what matters.

Conservation of Mechanical Energy

KEᵢ + PEᵢ = KE_f + PE_f    (conservative) W_nc = Δ(KE + PE)

Example

Block slides down a frictionless ramp from height h. At bottom: mgh = ½mv² ⇒ v = √(2gh). Mass cancels. With friction, W_friction = ΔME is negative and final speed is smaller.

When to use energy

Prefer energy methods when: you care about speeds at different heights, forces are unknown (normal, tension that do no work), or the path is complicated but only endpoints matter.

Power

P = W/t P = Fv cosθ

Energy Problem Checklist

Steps

1. Identify the system and whether friction/drag does work.
2. Choose PE = 0 location.
3. Write KE and PE at initial and final states.
4. If conservative: KEᵢ + PEᵢ = KE_f + PE_f.
5. If not: add W_nc on the appropriate side.
6. Solve symbolically; check units and limits (e.g. h → 0 ⇒ v → 0).
