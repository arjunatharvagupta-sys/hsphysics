12 — Optics

Geometric optics (mirrors & lenses) and wave optics (interference & diffraction).

Reflection & Mirrors

ELI5

Light bounces off a mirror so that the angle it hits equals the angle it leaves. Curved mirrors can focus light or make images that look bigger or smaller.

High School

Law of reflection: θᵢ = θᵣ. Plane mirrors form virtual, upright, same-size images. Spherical mirrors: 1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ. Concave mirrors can form real or virtual images; convex always virtual.

Professional

The law of reflection follows from Fermat’s principle (stationary optical path). The mirror equation is the paraxial approximation to the exact spherical reflecting surface. Sign conventions must be applied consistently for object and image distances and focal length.

θᵢ = θᵣ 1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ m = −dᵢ / dₒ

Refraction & Lenses

ELI5

Light bends when it enters a different material (water, glass) because it changes speed. Lenses use this bending to focus light and form images.

High School

Snell’s law: n₁ sinθ₁ = n₂ sinθ₂. Index of refraction n = c/v. Thin-lens equation is identical in form to the mirror equation. Converging lenses can form real or virtual images; diverging lenses form only virtual images.

Professional

Snell’s law is the boundary condition that follows from phase matching (or equivalently from Fermat’s principle) at an interface. The thin-lens equation is the first-order (paraxial) result for a lens of negligible thickness. Real systems require additional terms for spherical aberration, coma, and chromatic aberration.

n₁ sinθ₁ = n₂ sinθ₂ n = c / v 1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ

Interference & Diffraction

ELI5

Light can cancel itself or reinforce itself when two waves meet (interference). It also spreads out when it goes through a narrow opening (diffraction). These effects prove light is a wave.

High School

Double-slit: constructive interference when d sinθ = mλ. Single-slit diffraction minimum: a sinθ = mλ. Diffraction grating: d sinθ = mλ produces sharp bright fringes. Thin-film interference depends on path difference and phase changes upon reflection.

Professional

Interference is the linear superposition of coherent electromagnetic waves. Young’s double-slit pattern is the Fourier transform of two delta-function apertures. Diffraction in the far field is the Fourier transform of the aperture function. The intensity pattern for a grating of N slits becomes sharper as N increases, approaching a set of discrete diffraction orders.

Double slit: d sinθ = mλ Single slit min: a sinθ = mλ
