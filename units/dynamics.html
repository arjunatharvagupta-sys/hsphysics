03 — Dynamics & Newton’s Laws

What causes motion to change: forces, free-body diagrams, and the three laws that rule classical mechanics.

Force

ELI5

A force is a push or a pull. It can make something start moving, stop moving, speed up, slow down, or change direction.

High School

Force is a vector quantity measured in newtons (N). 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². Common forces: gravity (weight = mg), normal force, friction, tension, applied force, spring force. Net force is the vector sum of all forces acting on an object.

Professional

In Newtonian mechanics a force is any interaction that can cause an acceleration of a mass. It is a vector in three-dimensional Euclidean space and transforms accordingly under rotations and translations of the reference frame. The SI unit is the newton, defined via F = ma.

Newton’s First Law (Inertia)

ELI5

Things keep doing what they’re already doing. If something is sitting still, it stays still. If it’s moving, it keeps moving in a straight line at the same speed — unless a force pushes it or pulls it.

High School

An object at rest remains at rest, and an object in motion continues in uniform straight-line motion, unless acted upon by a net external force. This is the law of inertia. Mass is the measure of inertia.

Professional

In an inertial reference frame, the momentum of a body remains constant if the net external force vanishes. Equivalently, the acceleration is zero when ΣF = 0. The law defines the class of inertial frames and identifies mass as the quantitative measure of resistance to changes in velocity.

Newton’s Second Law

ELI5

The harder you push something, the faster its speed changes. The heavier it is, the less it speeds up for the same push. Force equals mass times acceleration.

High School

ΣF = ma. The net force on an object equals its mass times its acceleration. Acceleration is always in the direction of the net force. This is the central equation of dynamics and is used after drawing a free-body diagram and writing component equations.

Professional

The second law states that the time rate of change of momentum equals the net external force: ΣF = dp/dt. For constant mass this reduces to ΣF = ma. In component form in an inertial Cartesian frame it supplies three independent scalar equations that determine the motion once the forces are specified.

ΣF = ma ΣFₓ = maₓ    ΣFᵧ = maᵧ

Newton’s Third Law

ELI5

Whenever you push on something, it pushes back on you with exactly the same strength, just in the opposite direction. Forces always come in pairs.

High School

For every action force there is an equal and opposite reaction force. The two forces act on different objects and therefore never cancel each other on a free-body diagram of a single object.

Professional

If body A exerts a force F on body B, then body B exerts a force −F on body A. The forces are simultaneous, equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and collinear. They act on different bodies and therefore do not cancel in the equation of motion of either body alone.

Free-Body Diagrams & Weight

ELI5

A free-body diagram is a simple drawing that shows only the object you’re interested in and every push or pull acting on it as arrows. Weight is the pull of gravity — it always points down and equals mass times g.

High School

A free-body diagram isolates one object and represents every force acting on it as a vector arrow drawn from the center (or contact point). Weight W = mg acts at the center of mass, directed toward the center of Earth. The normal force is perpendicular to the surface. Tension acts along the string/rope away from the object.

Professional

A free-body diagram is the graphical embodiment of the isolation of a system for the application of Newton’s second law. All external forces (contact and long-range) are represented as localized vectors. Weight is the gravitational force mg in the uniform-field approximation; more generally it is the local value of the gravitational force including any centrifugal contributions in a rotating frame.

W = mg

Friction

ELI5

Friction is the force that tries to stop things from sliding. Static friction holds something still until you push hard enough; kinetic friction slows it down once it’s already sliding.

High School

Static friction: 0 ≤ fₛ ≤ μₛN; it matches the applied force up to its maximum. Kinetic friction: fₖ = μₖN, constant once sliding begins. Both oppose the relative motion (or attempted motion) at the surface. μ is the coefficient of friction, dimensionless.

Professional

The Coulomb model approximates dry friction: the static friction force satisfies |fₛ| ≤ μₛN and is determined by the requirement of zero relative acceleration while the inequality holds; the kinetic friction force has magnitude μₖN and direction opposite the relative velocity. Both coefficients depend on the pair of materials and surface conditions; the model neglects velocity dependence and deformation effects.

fₛ ≤ μₛ N fₖ = μₖ N
